01 / First principles

What is Model K?

Model K is an axiomatic proposal for the interpretation of Socionics grounded in a distinction between what a structure is in itself and the contingent manifestations through which it becomes observable.

Its philosophical orientation is substantially Aristotelian-Thomistic: explanation should not terminate in mnemonic descriptions of effects, but proceed toward the principles, relations, and causes by virtue of which those effects become intelligible.

Accordingly, Socionics is treated here primarily as an epistemological system. Its categories do not prescribe professions, personalities, habits, or stereotyped behaviors. They describe structurally distinct orientations through which aspects of reality are apprehended, organized, and interpreted.

Model K therefore attempts not to replace Socionics, but to recover its internal intelligibility: to ask not merely what a type appears to do, but what must be structurally true for such manifestations to become possible.

04 / Lexicon

Glossary

23 concepts

Object

The material referent of a description; numerical identity of an object does not entail identity among descriptions of it.

Aspect

The respect under which an object is apprehended. Distinct theories may address one object through different aspects.

Manifestation

An observable effect through which a structure becomes perceptible; contingent evidence, not the structure’s essence.

Primary cause

The underlying principle by which an effect becomes intelligible, prior to mnemonic or behavioral description.

Information metabolism

The structured apprehension and organization of aspects of reality through Socionic elements and their relations.

Static

An orientation toward stable regularities and conceptual contents abstracted from the complete empirical chain of effects.

Dynamic

Attention directed toward processes and the effective manifestations through which underlying structures become visible.

Mental

The conscious and organizing ring of direct recognition, especially articulated through the Ego.

Vital

The automatic, reactive background that sustains a dominant mode of interpretation.

Se

The principle by which the objective hierarchy of influence among entities within one field is apprehended.

Si

The principle by which internal sensory states are apprehended as direct bodily experience.

Ne

The principle by which possibilities are apprehended as static variations, abstracted from temporal direction.

Ni

The principle by which temporal tendencies and the probable direction of transformation are apprehended.

Te

The principle by which particular effects derived from causal regularities are apprehended.

Ti

The principle by which underlying causes and regularities are apprehended prior to their effects.

Fe

The principle by which the evaluation of an entity becomes communicable expression in the world.

Fi

The internal and categorical assessment of value as good or bad relative to the subject.

Correlation

A recurring association between independently produced classifications; it does not by itself establish identity.

Restriction

A correlational claim that excludes cross-theoretical combinations and therefore requires justification beyond association.

Transformation

The stronger correlational claim that one theoretical determination can alter or correct the conclusions of another system.

Coreference

The fact that two theories speak about the same object—nothing further follows necessarily from this fact.

Structural preservation

The requirement that translation preserve the relevant relations that make a category determinate within its system.

Interpretation

The intellectual organization of what is observed; it must remain constrained by the object rather than imposed upon it.
